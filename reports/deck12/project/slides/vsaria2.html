WHAT TO SAY: This slide answers the question we started with: when conditions change during a download, does our adaptive downloader do better than aria2? Each row is a situation where conditions change. Orange dots are our adaptive controllers, blue diamonds our best fixed setting. Left of the dashed line means faster than aria2. Top two rows, where the server gets faster during the download: yes, our adaptive controllers beat aria2. But the blue diamond is even further left: our fixed setting beat aria2 by more. So the win comes mostly from smaller pieces and request timing, not from adapting; a residual advantage remains unexplained. Bottom rows, where the server gets worse during the download (a cap switching on; bandwidth collapse, delay spikes, packet loss or throttling; six capped server variants): aria2 beat nearly every adaptive controller (on one of five trajectories the early agent was level with it, not slower). aria2’s big pieces start early and keep the good speed. IN SIMPLE WORDS: Against aria2, adapting helped only when the server sped up, and a fixed setting of ours did that even better. When things got worse, adapting did not help. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Adaptive controllers: the drop rule, the online tuner, the workload selector and the learning agents. • Collapse, delay, loss, throttle: five planned test scenarios where conditions change in phases, used with our early learning agent. • Separate experiments: the rows use different files and settings, so this is a guide to direction, not one combined measurement.
