WHAT TO SAY: Six directions for future work. First, a learner that picks N and S together as one action. Today, every move changes one dial; the rule-based controllers choose a target pair together but reach it one step at a time, and the joint agent we trained did not beat a good fixed setting. Second, live tests where delay and packet loss change during a download on a real path; we tested those only as fixed settings. Third, harder cases where the best action has a delayed or changing cost: sharing a download across several mirrors, restarting slow requests, and hedging stragglers. Fourth, a stall timeout, which cancels a request only when it stops making progress, instead of our current rule of no fixed deadline. Fifth, fairness: we measured speed, not how 16 connections share a link with other users. Sixth, more real data from more clients, networks and mirrors, including live mobile networks. We are also preparing a paper for IEEE Access. Thank you, and we are happy to take questions. IN SIMPLE WORDS: We showed when adapting helps and when it does not. Next we would test harder, more realistic conditions. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Stall timeout: cancelling a request only when it stops making progress, instead of after a fixed time. • Hedging: sending a duplicate request for a slow piece and keeping whichever finishes first. • Fairness: how evenly bandwidth is shared between different users on the same link. • IEEE Access: an open-access engineering journal that judges papers on soundness.
