Conclusion

Adapting pays only when concurrency has a cost that changes over time, and engine details come first

  • Against aria2: adaptive arms were faster only when the server rate rose, where a fixed setting of ours won by more; slower whenever the server degraded
  • Against a good fixed setting: adapting almost never won; a perfect controller could gain only 0.41 % on recorded mobile traces
  • Learning controllers: won on the server they trained for, and on some new conditions inside their training range; failed the no-harm test on all three real mirrors
  • Engine first: a request timeout and missing splitting of slow pieces explained aria2’s lead under the cap

Scope: controlled mechanisms, recorded traces and small real-server checks · not a validation on production servers

11

WHAT TO SAY: To sum up. Against aria2, our adaptive controllers were faster only when the server rate rose during the download, and even then a fixed setting of ours was faster still. When the server degraded, aria2 was faster than every adaptive controller. Against a well-chosen fixed setting, adapting almost never won, and on recorded mobile networks even a perfect controller could gain only 0.41 %. Our learning controllers won on the server they were trained for. With broader training, one agent also won on 3 of 4 new conditions inside its training ranges, but on none outside them. On all three real public mirrors they failed our no-harm test. And getting the download engine right mattered more than the controller: a whole-request timeout and the absence of splitting slow pieces explained aria2’s lead under the cap. In one sentence: changing connections and piece sizes during a download pays off only when using more connections has a cost that changes over time, and even then the engine matters first. All results are scoped to controlled mechanisms, recorded traces and small real-server checks. IN SIMPLE WORDS: Adapting is not automatically better. Measure whether there is anything to gain first, and fix the basics before adding intelligence. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Degraded server: a server whose speed falls during the download. • No-harm test: an adaptive controller passes if it is at most 5 % slower than the best fixed setting. • Straggler splitting: when one connection is idle at the end, it takes half of the slowest piece.
