WHAT TO SAY: Before the results, how to read them. Almost every result is a time ratio: our time divided by the other option’s time. Below 1 means we were faster: 0.80 means we took 80 % of their time, so 20 % less. Above 1 means slower. The numbers in brackets are the uncertainty range; if the whole range is below 1, we are confident we were faster. How we kept it fair: we run every option back to back in a random order, round after round, so if the laptop is a bit slower at some moment, every option is affected equally. We wrote down each experiment plan, including the pass/fail rules, before running it. And before building any learning controller, we measured the ceiling: how much a perfect controller could possibly gain. Finally, every downloaded file is checked byte for byte, and a script recomputes every number in our paper from the raw data. IN SIMPLE WORDS: Fair races, rules written before we saw the results, and numbers anyone can recheck. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Round (“block”): one run of every option, in random order. • Uncertainty range (“95 % interval”): where the true value very likely lies, worked out by resampling our rounds (“bootstrap”) or, for some headline results, by a t-interval on the log ratio. • Geometric mean: the average we use for ratios; it treats “half the time” and “twice the time” as equal and opposite. • Experiment plan (“protocol”): written and fingerprinted before the first run; any later change is added as a dated note. • Perfect controller (“oracle”) and ceiling (“headroom”): see slide 5. • Claims checker: our script, tools/claims_check.py.
