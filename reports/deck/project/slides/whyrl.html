Why reinforcement learning did not win

The learner was not the bottleneck. The opportunity was.

Little to gain: even a perfect controller gained almost nothing on recorded networks

Simple rules suffice: where there was a gain, a simple rule already did well

Engine first: timeouts and splitting changed more than any controller

37

WHAT TO SAY: Why did the AI not win? Not because our AI was bad. Because there was very little for any controller to gain. First, the ceiling was low: the oracle, the perfect controller, gained almost nothing on real traces and real servers. The best number of connections is roughly “enough to fill the pipe”; when the network slows down, that number stays about the same. Second, where we built real gains, a simple one-line rule already captured them. Third, fixing the engine (the timeout and straggler splitting) changed results more than any controller did. IN SIMPLE WORDS: You cannot win a race when the finish line is where you already are. A smarter driver does not help on a road with no shortcuts. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Bottleneck: the thing that limits speed; here, the opportunity itself, not the learning method. • Ceiling: the best result possible, given by the oracle. • Threshold rule: a rule that acts when a measurement crosses a fixed value, like “speed fell below half”.
