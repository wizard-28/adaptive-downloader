Limitations

What these results do not show

  • Constructed servers: the positive adaptive results hold on a server model we designed
  • One machine: loopback with netem, one aria2 version (1.37.0)
  • Small real-network studies: one client, one primary network
  • Replayed traces, not live mobile networks
  • Narrow AI tests: two kinds of learner; our best agent is one chosen training run
  • Testbed noise: tight intervals measure the emulator, not the world
  • No stall timeout yet in the fixed engine
  • Speed, not fairness: 16 connections take more of a shared link

WHAT TO SAY: Every study has limits, and it is better that we say them first. The servers where adapting helped are models we built; real servers may behave differently. All the controlled tests ran on one laptop, with the client and server on the same machine. Our real-internet tests were small: one home connection. The mobile tests replayed recordings rather than using a live phone network. We tried two families of learning methods, and v2 is one chosen version. Because our test server is very consistent, our uncertainty ranges are narrow; they show the noise of the test setup, not of the real world. With the 30-second limit removed, a completely stuck connection would wait forever; a smarter “no progress” timeout is future work. And we measured speed, not fairness: 16 connections take a bigger share of a shared link from other users. IN SIMPLE WORDS: Our results are careful and honest, but they come from a controlled lab plus small real-world checks. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Loopback: client and server on the same computer. • Seed: the random starting point of training. • Stall timeout: cancelling a request only when it stops making progress, instead of after a fixed time. • Fairness: how evenly bandwidth is shared between different users on the same link. • Predeclared: plans written and fingerprinted before running, though not registered with an outside service.
