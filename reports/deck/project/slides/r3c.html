WHAT TO SAY: Once the engine was fixed, a fixed setting of 16 connections with big 32 MiB pieces became the best under the cap. It beat every adaptive controller: the drop-rule controller was 7 % slower, and the workload selector 23 % slower. We also rebuilt the idea of a recent published downloader, FastBioDL, which nudges the number of connections up or down every 2 seconds. It was about 21 % slower than even a fixed setting with the same 1 MiB pieces. It reacted to the cap quickly, in about 2 seconds, but it starts with 4 connections and needed 8 seconds to climb to 16 when there was no cap. Why does the big-piece setting win? It starts all 16 big pieces before the cap arrives at 8 seconds, and each piece keeps the fast speed it started with. IN SIMPLE WORDS: The earlier 23 % win was only against small-piece settings on our old engine. With a sound engine and a good fixed setting, adapting lost. Speed of reaction was not the problem; there was little to gain. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Best fixed setting: the fastest fixed N and S we measured in that test. It is chosen after the runs, so it is a strong comparison. • Online tuner: a controller that keeps nudging N up or down based on measured speed. We wrote our own version of FastBioDL’s idea, not their code. • Workload selector: watches the first seconds, guesses the kind of server, then picks a preset setting.
