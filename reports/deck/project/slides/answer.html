WHAT TO SAY: Here is the answer before the details, in three numbers. 62.4 %: on a server whose speed changes, our downloader with one fixed setting took 62.4 % less time than aria2. But this was not adaptation; it won largely because of when its requests started. 0.41 %: on real recorded mobile networks, even a perfect controller that knows the future could save only 0.41 % on average. There was almost nothing to gain, even though the speed swings wildly. 0 of 3: when we ran our adaptive controllers on three real public download servers, they were slower than a good fixed setting on all three. IN SIMPLE WORDS: Adapting beat aria2 only when the server sped up during the download, and even then a fixed setting of ours beat aria2 by more. Against a well-chosen fixed setting, adapting almost never won. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Perfect controller (“oracle”): an imaginary controller that knows the future and always picks the best setting. Nobody can build one; we use it as a ceiling. If even it gains nothing, no real controller can. • Public server (“mirror”): a server that hosts copies of files for anyone to download, here the Linux kernel downloads.
