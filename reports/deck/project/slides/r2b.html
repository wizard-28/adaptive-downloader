WHAT TO SAY: We did not stop at mobile networks. We looked at many other kinds of bad conditions, to find one where the best setting really changes during a download. Simple adaptive rules on the slow-then-fast server did change settings, but took 2.3 times as long as the best fixed setting. Competing traffic on a shared link, bursty packet loss, servers that throttle each request, changing piece sizes on a steady server: none of them gave a meaningful gain, because the best setting stayed the same throughout. One warning story: one simulator design showed 17.6 % to gain, but when we checked on the real test setup, it was not there. The simulator had invented a penalty that the real system does not have. After that, we always checked simulators against real runs before trusting them. IN SIMPLE WORDS: We searched hard for a situation where adapting pays. We could not find a natural one, so next we built one. TERMS ON THIS SLIDE: • Heavy traffic on a shared link: we ran a traffic generator (iperf3) alongside the download to imitate other users. • Burst loss: packets lost in bunches rather than randomly. • Simulator: a program that imitates the download so we can test or train quickly without real downloads.
